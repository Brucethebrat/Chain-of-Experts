The Tolerance Design Optimization Problem aims to assign manufacturing tolerances to components in a mechanical assembly in order to minimize the total cost. This total cost includes both manufacturing cost and quality loss, while ensuring that the product meets overall variation constraints. The problem involves a set of components arranged in a linear stack-up. Each component has a tolerance T_i​ that can be adjusted within allowable bounds. The total cost of the assembly consists of Manufacturing Cost and quality loss. The cost of manufacturing is inversely proportional to the square of each components’ tolerance. The assembly quality loss is negatively affected by variability in dimensions. The quality loss is proportional to the sum of the squares of the tolerances across all components. Each tolerance TiT_iTi​ must be greater than or equal to a minimum manufacturable value t_min​. The total assembly-level tolerance, modeled using the Root-Sum-of-Squares (RSS) method, must be within a specified limit T_max. The problem seeks to find the most cost-effective assignment of tolerances to components that minimizes both production costs and quality degradation, while ensuring the final product meets engineering performance requirements.
