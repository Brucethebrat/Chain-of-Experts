You are designing a mechanical assembly composed of three linearly stacked components, labeled A, B, and C. Each component must be manufactured with a specific tolerance, namely a permissible range of deviation from its nominal dimension. The challenge is to determine the optimal tolerances for each component to achieve a balance between manufacturing cost and product quality. Each component's tolerance affects both cost and quality. The manufacturing cost of a component increases as the assigned tolerance becomes tighter. This cost is inversely proportional to the square of the tolerance. The sensitivity of cost with respect to tolerance for each component is represented by coefficients given in the input as "k". The product quality degrades as tolerances become looser. To capture this, a quality penalty is added, which is proportional to the sum of the tolerances. The total objective is a weighted sum of the total cost and the total quality penalty. The relative importance of these two aspects is controlled by the weights "alpha" (for cost) and "beta" (for quality), provided in the input. Each component’s tolerance must be greater than or equal to a specified minimum value, given by "t_min". The combined variation from all components must not exceed a total allowable limit on the assembly’s variation. This is defined using the root-sum-square (RSS) of the three tolerances and is constrained by the value given in the input as "T_max". Using the input values "k", "t_min", "T_max", "alpha", and "beta", determine the set of tolerances (output: "tolerances") that best balances manufacturing cost and quality, and report the final value of the total objective (output: "total_objective").
